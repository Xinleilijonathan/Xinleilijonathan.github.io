AboutResearch focus

I am a final-semester M.S. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Northeastern University (Seattle), graduating December 2026. My current research studies how vision-language-action models adapt to real robots: how teleoperation quality, demonstration count, and camera setup shape what a fine-tuned policy can do, and where it fails under distribution shift.

For my PhD, I want to build embodied agents that learn from limited real-world data. ThesisM.S. thesis

Simulation-to-Real Demonstration Scaling for Vision-Language-Action Robot Learning on a UR12e Platform

in progress · Dec 2026
Advisor: Prof. Xian Li · Northeastern University, Electrical and Computer Engineering

VLA models connect images, language, and robot actions, but how well they work on a new robot depends on the quantity, quality, diversity, and embodiment match of the post-training data. Real demonstrations are slow to collect, and failed policies can damage hardware. My thesis builds a reproducible simulation-to-real pipeline for a UR12e with a Robotiq Hand-E gripper, and uses it to measure what drives real-robot success: how many demonstrations, which ones, in which action space, and from which camera views.

Expected contributions
  • A reproducible simulation-to-real VLA training pipeline for the UR12e.
  • LeRobot-compatible dataset adapters and manifests for DROID, RoboMIND, Berkeley UR5, and RoboLabSim.
  • Demonstration-scaling curves that relate task performance to data quantity and compute.
  • A controlled comparison of joint-position and end-effector-pose action representations.
  • A demonstration-quality filtering and weighting study.
  • A multi-view and simulation-to-real evaluation with quantitative failure analysis.

What early experiments showed

Initial post-training with a Cosmos3-Nano action policy on RoboLabSim, Berkeley UR5, DROID, and RoboMIND validated the training and adapter workflow. It also showed that a good training loss does not by itself produce reliable robot behavior.

  • Joint-position action policies were more stable than end-effector-pose policies.
  • Grasp precision and depth alignment were the main failure modes.
  • Gripper open/close timing was not learned consistently.
  • Large datasets contain jitter, failed grasps, and recovery motions that can lower policy quality.
  • Small-subset tests help with debugging but cannot pick the best data mixture.
Research questions
  1. How does performance scale with demonstration count when the model and evaluation protocol stay fixed?
  2. Does joint-position control beat end-effector-pose control once dataset size and compute are matched?
  3. Can trajectory-quality filtering or weighted sampling beat training on every demonstration?
  4. How do simulation-only, real-only, and mixed datasets affect transfer to the UR12e?
  5. Do front-camera, wrist-camera, and combined views give different results on grasping and precision tasks?
  6. How sensitive are the conclusions to random seed, task difficulty, object perturbation, and embodiment?
Experimental design
Action space
Joint actions (Δq, Δg) against end-effector actions (Δx, Δr, Δg), with the same observations, data, and evaluation.
Data scale
20, 50, 100, 200, 500, and 1,000 demonstrations, plus percentage subsets of large datasets, to find where performance plateaus.
Data quality
Q = w₁·Ssuccess + w₂·Ssmooth + w₃·Sconsistency + w₄·Ssafety. No filtering vs. hard threshold vs. quality-weighted sampling.
Views and data mix
Front only, wrist only, and both. Simulation only, real only, and mixed.
Robustness
Object-position shifts, distractors, lighting, and camera perturbations, with multiple random seeds per key condition.
Metrics
Success rate with confidence intervals, partial progress, execution time, failure rates by type, and smoothness J = Σ ‖at+1 − 2at + at−1‖². At least 20 real rollouts per key final condition.

PhDFuture directions

My thesis asks which data makes a VLA policy work on a real robot. In a PhD, I want to make robots need less of it, by learning beyond their demonstrations.

direction

World models for evaluation

Learn action-conditioned video world models from robot data, and test whether imagined rollouts rank policies the way real trials do.

direction

RL fine-tuning of VLAs

Improve a VLA's action head with reinforcement learning inside a learned world model, using few real rollouts to correct model errors.

direction

Data-efficient teleoperation

Use model uncertainty to decide which demonstrations to collect next, so each hour of teleoperation improves the policy more.
