AboutResearch focus

I am a final-semester M.S. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Northeastern University (Seattle), graduating December 2026. My current research studies how vision-language-action models adapt to real robots: how teleoperation quality, demonstration count, and camera setup shape what a fine-tuned policy can do, and where it fails under distribution shift.

For my PhD, I want to build embodied agents that learn from limited real-world data. I am especially interested in combining VLA policies with reinforcement learning and learned world models, so a robot can predict the outcome of its actions and improve past its demonstrations. Before robotics, I earned a B.S. in Computer Science at Wenzhou-Kean University and published work on vision-based flame and smoke detection.

Simulation-Based VLA Policy Development

2026

Fine-tuned NVIDIA Cosmos3-Nano on 250 Berkeley UR5 bottle episodes (21K valid 16-step windows) on H200 GPUs, with a 10-D action adapter for translation, 6-D rotation, and gripper. Dataset-specific action normalization gave the largest single gain. I then connected policy outputs to Isaac Sim UR10 with Lula FK/IK, corrected a 90.04° frame mismatch, and completed a verified bottle-to-pot pick-and-place baseline.

An Enhanced YOLOv8 for Flame and Smoke Detection with Dilated Convolution and Image Dehazing
Leibiao Hu, Chang Lu*, Xinlei Li, Yuxiang Zhu, Yipeng Lu, Sujatha Krishnamoorthy
Proc. SPIE 12970, Fourth International Conference on Signal Processing and Computer Science (SPCS 2023), 129702I

FSD-YOLOv8 adds an AOD-Net dehazing stage and dilated convolutions to YOLOv8 to separate smoke from fog and haze. On 95,314 FASDD_CV images it reaches 0.92 mAP and 0.91 F1, against 0.85 and 0.84 for baseline YOLOv8, and generalizes to Fire-Net and Wildfire Smoke & Fire.
